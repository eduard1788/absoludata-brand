# Absoludata — Customer Acquisition & Website Update Plan

**Purpose:** This document is the working brief for GitHub Copilot and the founder. Use it to update the Absoludata website and execute a practical customer-acquisition process.

**Company:** Absoludata  
**Website:** https://absoludata.com  
**Business model:** Consulting and professional services — not a SaaS product  
**Initial market:** Mexico, with potential expansion to Spanish-speaking Latin America and English-speaking caribean countries.  
**Core expertise:** Data engineering, AWS data platforms, data pipeline reliability, modernization, integrations, and AI-enabled workflow automation

---

## 1. Business positioning

### Recommended positioning

**Absoludata is an AI-enabled data engineering consultancy that helps small and mid-sized businesses build reliable data platforms, modernize cloud infrastructure, integrate business systems, and automate data-intensive workflows.**

Suggested homepage headline:

> **Turn Your Business Data Into a Competitive Advantage**

Suggested supporting copy:

> We help small and mid-sized businesses build reliable data platforms, modernize cloud infrastructure, connect business systems, and automate complex workflows with AI.

Keep the language concrete and business-oriented. Explain what Absoludata helps clients accomplish rather than relying on broad claims such as “digital transformation” or “AI innovation.”

### Important positioning principles

- Present Absoludata as a **consultancy and delivery partner**, not a SaaS product.
- Lead with data engineering and AWS capabilities; present AI as a practical tool for automation and problem-solving.
- Emphasize reliable systems, better data availability, less manual work, maintainability, and delivery capacity.
- Avoid claiming guaranteed savings, revenue growth, or performance improvements.
- Only publish customer names, testimonials, metrics, logos, and case studies when they are real and approved for publication.
- Keep the website suitable for both direct customers and potential white-label/implementation partners.
- Use clear, straightforward language; avoid unnecessary technical jargon.

## 3. Initial service offers

Keep the offers concrete. Do not lead with an unlimited list of capabilities.

### Offer 1 — Data Engineering Assessment

**Purpose:** A paid, defined first engagement for clients who know they have data-platform or pipeline problems but need help identifying priorities.

**Possible scope**
- Review current architecture, pipelines, integrations, and operational workflow.
- Identify reliability, performance, data-quality, maintainability, and cost-efficiency issues.
- Identify appropriate automation opportunities.
- Prioritize recommendations by business impact and effort.
- Deliver a findings document and review meeting.

**Initial pricing hypothesis:** USD $750–$1,500, depending on scope and access requirements.

**Duration hypothesis:** 5–7 business days after required access and information are available.

These figures are initial pricing hypotheses, not verified market rates. Adjust based on project complexity, client budget, delivery effort, and feedback.

Do not provide a large unpaid assessment. If a client already has a well-defined problem, consider proposing a scoped implementation directly.

### Offer 2 — Data Platform / Pipeline Implementation

Potential work:
- Build or improve AWS data pipelines.
- Integrate business systems.
- Implement data processing and orchestration.
- Improve reliability, monitoring, and maintainability.
- Deliver a defined migration or modernization workstream.

**Initial project pricing hypothesis:** USD $4,000–$15,000+, depending on scope and complexity. Do not quote without understanding requirements, risks, access, and acceptance criteria.

### Offer 3 — Ongoing Data Engineering Support

Potential work:
- Pipeline monitoring and issue investigation.
- Incremental reliability improvements.
- Technical maintenance and optimization.
- Specialist engineering support for an internal team.
- Planned monthly delivery capacity.

**Initial retainer hypothesis:** USD $1,500–$4,000/month, with scope and service expectations defined in writing.

Do not promise 24/7 coverage, guaranteed response times, or production support unless staffing and operational arrangements genuinely support it.

### Offer 4 — Specialist Delivery Partner for Agencies

Potential work:
- AWS data engineering capacity for an agency’s client projects.
- White-label or joint delivery.
- Defined implementation tasks and technical reviews.

Agree in writing on scope, responsibilities, confidentiality, intellectual property, client communication, delivery acceptance, and payment terms.

## 5. Prospect research: build a small, evidence-based list

The goal is not to collect hundreds of names. It is to identify companies with a plausible need, a reason to talk now, and a relevant person to contact.

### Start with two separate lists

- **Direct prospects:** Start with 30 companies.
- **Potential delivery partners:** Start with 20 agencies or consultancies.

Begin in Mexico. Expand to other Spanish-speaking Latin American markets after learning which segment and message generate meaningful conversations.

### Free research sources

Use a free LinkedIn account initially, supplemented by:
- Company websites and service pages.
- LinkedIn company pages and professional profiles.
- LinkedIn Jobs and public job descriptions.
- Company press releases and announcements.
- Public professional posts and event speaker pages.
- Industry events, meetups, and relevant business communities.
- Search engines.

Paid LinkedIn features are optional. Do not buy Sales Navigator before validating the process.

### Example search queries

Use these as starting points and adjust them by location, industry, and language:

- `"Data Engineer" AWS Mexico`
- `Airflow OR Snowflake OR dbt Mexico`
- `logistics OR ecommerce OR manufacturing Mexico`
- `"software development" OR "cloud consulting" Mexico`
- Spanish-language equivalents, including “ingeniería de datos,” “consultoría AWS,” and “integración de datos.”

Search results are discovery leads, not proof of customer demand.

### Especially useful signal: hiring

For each relevant Data Engineer, Analytics Engineer, or cloud/data job:
1. Identify the employer.
2. Read the job description.
3. Record relevant technologies or initiatives explicitly mentioned.
4. Review the company website and business model.
5. Identify the likely technical decision-maker.
6. Consider whether Absoludata could offer complementary project support.
7. Record the job URL and date checked.

A hiring signal may indicate active data work, but it does not prove that the company wants external help.

### Research each company in 5–10 minutes

For each company, record:
1. What the company does and its industry.
2. Approximate size and geography.
3. One verified need/timing signal.
4. The possible problem Absoludata might help with.
5. One primary decision-maker and, if useful, one secondary contact.
6. An appropriate contact route.
7. Source URLs and dates.

Use this sentence as a quality test:

> “I believe this company is worth contacting because [specific evidence], which may indicate [potential business problem].”

If the sentence cannot be supported by observable facts, research further or deprioritize the company.

### Find the right decision-maker

| Potential problem | Likely roles |
|---|---|
| Pipeline reliability/performance | Data Engineering Manager, Head of Data |
| Data platform migration | CTO, Data Platform Lead |
| System integration complexity | Engineering Manager, CTO |
| Analytics/reporting foundations | Head of Analytics, Analytics Engineering Manager |
| Specialist delivery capacity | Agency Founder, Delivery Director |

For small businesses, the CTO or founder may own several responsibilities. Prioritize the person closest to the problem, not automatically the most senior executive.

### Prioritization scorecard

Score each category from 0 to 2:

- **Problem signal:** 0 = none, 1 = plausible, 2 = clear evidence.
- **Timing:** 0 = no trigger, 1 = general growth, 2 = recent relevant event.
- **Service fit:** 0 = weak, 1 = adjacent, 2 = directly matches an offer.
- **Access:** 0 = no contact identified, 1 = relevant contact identified, 2 = relevant contact plus a credible route.

Suggested internal sorting rule:
- 6–8: prioritize for outreach.
- 4–5: investigate further.
- 0–3: defer.

This is an operational heuristic, not a validated prediction of sales success.

## 7. Outreach to people outside the existing network

Use the most appropriate channel based on the relationship and available public information.

### Route A — Mutual introduction

Check whether former colleagues, clients, professional contacts, vendors, or acquaintances know the prospect. Ask for a short introduction and explain the specific reason for the conversation.

### Route B — LinkedIn connection request

Keep it brief and specific. Do not send a full sales pitch in the invitation.

Example:

> Hola, [Nombre]. Vi que estás liderando el área de ingeniería en [Empresa]. Trabajo en ingeniería de datos sobre AWS y automatización de procesos. Me pareció interesante conectar con personas que están trabajando en estos temas.

After acceptance, use the verified company signal to start a relevant conversation. Acceptance is not an invitation to send a long pitch.

### Route C — Business email

Use an appropriate public business contact route and personalize the message.

**Subject:** Apoyo especializado en ingeniería de datos para [Empresa]

> Hola, [Nombre]:
>
> Soy el fundador de Absoludata. Ayudamos a equipos a construir y modernizar plataformas de datos sobre AWS, mejorar la confiabilidad de sus pipelines y automatizar procesos de datos.
>
> Vi que [observación concreta y verificada]. Me pareció que podría haber oportunidades para simplificar algunas integraciones o acelerar proyectos de datos, aunque no sé si actualmente sea una prioridad para ustedes.
>
> ¿Tendría sentido conversar 15 minutos para conocer cómo están abordando estos retos?
>
> Saludos,  
> [Nombre]  
> Absoludata

### Route D — Professional communities and events

Participate in AWS and data engineering events, relevant meetups, webinars, and professional groups. Contribute useful technical insights and follow up with people who show genuine interest.

### Outreach examples by signal

**Hiring signal**

> Hola, [Nombre]. Vi que [Empresa] está ampliando su equipo de datos y que el puesto menciona [tecnología confirmada]. Soy fundador de Absoludata y me especializo en ingeniería de datos sobre AWS. Quería preguntarte si, además de las contrataciones internas, en ocasiones consideran apoyo externo para proyectos específicos o migraciones. ¿Es algo que tenga sentido para ustedes?

**Business expansion**

> Hola, [Nombre]. Vi el anuncio de [Empresa] sobre [expansión o iniciativa concreta]. Trabajo con plataformas de datos, integraciones y automatización sobre AWS desde Absoludata. Me interesó conocer cómo están abordando la integración y disponibilidad de datos a medida que crecen. ¿Quién suele liderar ese tipo de iniciativas en su equipo?

**Potential agency partner**

> Hola, [Nombre]. Estuve revisando los servicios de [Agencia] y vi que trabajan con [servicio confirmado]. Estoy desarrollando Absoludata, una consultoría especializada en ingeniería de datos sobre AWS y automatización. Me gustaría explorar si podemos complementar sus proyectos cuando necesiten capacidad especializada en pipelines, integraciones o plataformas de datos. ¿Te interesaría una conversación breve para conocernos?

Only mention technologies, initiatives, or services that have actually been verified. If no relevant trigger is available, use a straightforward introduction instead of inventing one.

## 8. Follow-up cadence

Use a consistent, respectful cadence. Update the tracker after each interaction.

- **Day 0:** Send the initial personalized message.
- **Day 4–5:** Brief follow-up if there is no response.
- **Day 10–14:** Send one additional message with a relevant example, insight, or question.
- **Day 20–25:** Send a polite final follow-up, then stop.

If a person declines, asks not to be contacted, or indicates that the topic is not relevant, record that and stop outreach. Avoid contacting several people at the same company simultaneously without a reason.

### Example follow-up

> Hola, [Nombre]. Retomo brevemente mi mensaje anterior. Me interesó [señal o tema específico] porque es un área en la que trabajamos desde Absoludata. Si no es una prioridad ahora, no hay problema. Gracias por tu tiempo.

Do not use false urgency or pretend to have a relationship that does not exist.

## 9. Discovery calls and qualification

A first conversation should be about understanding the problem, not delivering a generic pitch.

Suggested 20–30 minute agenda:
1. Understand the business and the person's responsibilities.
2. Ask about current data systems, workflows, and architecture at an appropriate level.
3. Understand bottlenecks, reliability issues, manual work, or delivery constraints.
4. Explore the operational or business impact.
5. Ask why the issue matters now and what happens if it remains unresolved.
6. Understand timeline, stakeholders, budget process, security requirements, and access constraints.
7. Agree on a sensible next step.

A qualified opportunity should have a concrete problem, a reason to address it, an identifiable owner, and a plausible approval/budget path.

Possible next steps:
- Paid Data Engineering Assessment.
- Clearly scoped implementation.
- Follow-up technical discovery with relevant stakeholders.
- Partner discussion with a consulting agency.

Do not automatically discount. If the budget is limited, reduce scope or split the work into phases.

### Proposal checklist

Send proposals promptly, ideally within 1–2 business days after enough information is available. Include:
- Client problem and desired outcome.
- Scope and deliverables.
- Timeline and dependencies.
- Fee and payment milestones.
- Assumptions and exclusions.
- Required access, security, and client responsibilities.
- Acceptance criteria.
- Next steps and proposal validity, if applicable.

## 12. LinkedIn paid plans

Start with a free LinkedIn account.

A free account is sufficient to begin:
- Search for companies and people.
- Review public company pages and many profiles.
- Search jobs and identify hiring signals.
- Send connection requests, subject to platform limits.
- Publish content and follow up with connections.

Paid plans may offer additional filters, saved lead lists, prospect insights, or InMail features. Features, eligibility, limits, and prices can change, so verify current terms before subscribing.

**Decision rule:** Consider Sales Navigator only after you have tried the free workflow and can identify a specific bottleneck that advanced search, saved lead lists, or alerts would solve. A paid subscription does not guarantee prospect access or replies.

After the manual workflow proves useful, consider a lightweight internal research assistant built with Python and an LLM.

Possible workflow:

3. Summarize potential need signals with source URLs and dates.
4. Suggest likely decision-maker roles.

6. Save findings to a spreadsheet or CRM.
7. Require human approval before any outreach.

Do not automate bulk LinkedIn messaging or indiscriminately collect personal contact data. Respect source terms, platform rules, privacy obligations, and opt-out requests. Automate research and organization first; keep outreach personal.
